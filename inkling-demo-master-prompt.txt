MASTER PROMPT — Cafe Cursor Shanghai video cut (Inkling 2 Small demo)

Use this single prompt for the whole job: scan the footage, pick the
moments, and cut the clips. Everything the video needs is in here.

## 1. What this is

You are cutting a tech meetup into short clips for social media. The
event is Cafe Cursor Shanghai, Sunday, July 19, 2026, 10:00-17:00, in a
cafe on Yanqing Road, Shanghai, under plane trees. It ran during WAIC
2026. There was no keynote and no fixed agenda. In the morning people
sat down, got their questions answered, and worked side by side. In the
afternoon the demo area opened and whoever built something stood up and
showed it.

## 2. The story (tell it whole)

Morning sun falls through the plane trees. The cafe door is open, a
small blackboard standing outside. People walk in carrying laptops,
carrying what they are working on, carrying what they want to talk
about. On the tables are cup drinks sponsored by Cursor, and the room
is beautifully arranged.

In the morning everyone settles in, gets their questions answered, and
works side by side. In the afternoon the demo area opens, and whoever
built something stands up and shows it.

Then, in the afternoon, the plum rain arrives all at once. A heavy
downpour, the street empty in minutes. At the door the volunteers are
rushed off their feet — one checking people in, one carrying coffee
through the crowd. But nobody leaves. Everyone crowds inside, and the
room gets fuller.

Inside, the laptops are open and the coffee is steaming. Someone stands
up and talks about what they built, for minutes at a time, and the room
laughs, picks up the thread, asks questions. Music is the main character
of that afternoon: a foreigner plays the guitar, there is piano, and
someone plays a piece he wrote for his girlfriend. Someone else uses AI
to solve a small problem in everyday life. The harder the rain falls,
the warmer the room gets. The questions that usually leave a person
stuck alone — here, someone always catches them.

In the evening the rain is still falling and nobody wants to leave. The
next day, the whole day is cut into three short clips, thirty to ninety
seconds each. But what is worth keeping was never the clips themselves.
It is the room full of people in the sound of rain. It is the moment
someone shows what they made. It is the question that follows.

At the end, everyone stands together for a group photo. The camera
flashes back: morning sun, afternoon rain, laughter in the room — and
the line that holds the day together. Someone always catches your
question.

## 3. Atmosphere and feeling

The atmosphere is rain, warm light, laughter. The feeling is being
caught, not wanting to leave. The room is busy: people coming and
going, the crowd flowing, volunteers rushing, rain outside, nobody
leaves. For each person's showcase: pick the best part, one with a
response from the room (laughter, applause, someone picking up the
thread), plus the interactive Q&A parts (someone asks, someone answers,
someone follows up).

## 4. What to look for, in priority order

1. The foreigner playing guitar
2. The piece someone wrote for his girlfriend (a song? a letter? a few
   lines? note down exactly what it is)
3. Piano
4. Guest showcase monologues (someone standing up to talk about what
   they built)
5. Follow-up questions and answers (someone asks, someone catches the
   question)
6. Laughter and cheering (moments of joy)
7. The rain (downpour outside the window, rain sounds, people crowding
   inside)
8. Volunteers rushing around (checking people in at the door, carrying
   coffee)
9. The group photo
10. Any other moment with a clear takeaway, a live demo, or a surprising
    point

## 5. Structure

Tell the day across three time nodes: morning / afternoon / evening.
Video is the main thread; photos cut in as 1–2 second flashbacks at
the end of each node (never long holds — a photo that sits for seconds
drags the pace). Structure: recap → highlights → group photo. First
tell the day in order (recap, about 2 minutes), then the best highlight
moments (3 clips, 30-90 seconds each), then the group photo to close,
with a flashback.

## 6. Style rules

- Backgrounds stay soft throughout: shallow depth of field, soft
  light, never stealing focus.
- Present-day footage: clean, warm, normal 16:9.
- Flashback: film grain, a little light leak, 4:3 black bars, like old
  footage.
- Background music: chosen well, kept low, never covering voices.
- Backgrounds consistent, camera steady, never dizzying.
- No heavy effects yet — only a touch of vintage faded-color filter.

## 7. Music and timing (main track)

Main track: KOYO — "Timing" (타이밍), 3:16, 99 BPM, Korean OST
(EXchange2 Pt. 4 "My Heart Flutter, Again", 2022). Soft ballad; the
lyric is literally about perfect timing. One beat = 0.61 s, one bar =
2.42 s, one phrase (8 bars) = 19.4 s.

Long take: one continuous moving shot glides through the cafe (door →
tables → demo area → group photo) as the spine of the recap. Cut-ins
land on the song's phrase boundaries: 0:00, 0:19, 0:39, 0:58, 1:18,
1:37, 1:56, 2:16, 2:35, 2:55, 3:14. Each cut-in is a close-up (guitar
hands, piano keys, a laughing face, rain on the window), then back to
the long take. Boundaries assume the first downbeat at 0:00; exact
verse/chorus times to be marked from the audio file.

Alternates: 88rising & RHYME SO — "Just Used Music Again" (3:31, 123
BPM, deep house, phrase every 15.6 s); Yerin Baek — "Another season
with you" (3:30, 120 BPM, phrase every 16.0 s); Cloud Nothings —
"Stay Useless" (2:47, 169 BPM, phrase every 11.4 s, highlight bursts
only).

## 8. How to pick the clips

Only pick moments that make sense without the rest of the talk. Prefer
moments with a clear takeaway, a live demo, or a surprising point.
Prefer moments where a guest is presenting their own work in their own
words (a showcase monologue). Prefer moments that include audience
response (laughter, applause, someone picking up the thread) or
follow-up questions. Do not cut mid-sentence; extend to the nearest
sentence boundary if needed. Timestamps must match the transcript.

## 9. Rules

Mark anything uncertain instead of guessing. For example, for the piece
for his girlfriend, write down exactly what you hear; if you cannot
hear it clearly, write "unclear, to verify".

## 10. Output

- Total duration of the footage
- A moment table grouped by morning / afternoon / evening. For each
  moment: start and end timestamps, one line on what happened, why it
  is worth keeping (moving / funny / a clear takeaway / a live demo),
  who is speaking (if you can tell), whether the room responded
  (laughter, applause, someone picking up the thread, follow-up
  questions)
- **The main deliverable: a 2-minute recap plan.** One continuous
  long take as the spine (door → tables → demo area → group photo),
  with cut-ins on the song's phrase boundaries (0:00, 0:19, 0:39,
  0:58, 1:18, 1:37, 1:56, 2:16, 2:35, 2:55, 3:14). List the moments
  in order, each with a start and end timestamp, and say which
  phrase-boundary cut-in it lands on. The recap is about 2 minutes
  total.
- Bonus, for later (not needed for the demo): the 3 most recommended
  clips (30-90 seconds each), each with a start and end timestamp, a
  short title, and one line on why it works

Note: you output timestamps and a plan, not a video file. The cutting
is done downstream by ffmpeg from your timestamps — you pick the
moments, the cutter cuts them.

## 11. Narration for the 2-minute recap (voiceover, spoken in Chinese)

The recap has a voiceover, timed to the phrase grid. It plays over the
long take; room audio dips under it, music stays low. Read it exactly
as written, with pauses between lines — the footage carries the gaps.

- 0:00–0:19 (morning): On July 19 this year, at a café on Yanqing Road in Shanghai, under the plane trees — one table, one coffee — we held a Cafe Cursor. No keynote, no fixed agenda.
- 0:19–0:39 (morning): In the morning everyone sat down, asked questions, and coworked a little; the afternoon was the demo area — whoever built something showed it and hunted for bugs.
- 0:39–0:58 (rain): Then in the afternoon, the plum rain came down all at once — the street emptied in an instant, but inside it was packed. The volunteers were overwhelmed at the door, one checking people in, one carrying coffee through the crowd. But nobody left.
- 0:58–1:18 (music): What moved people most inside was the music: a foreigner played a piece on guitar, there was piano, and someone shared something he wrote for his girlfriend.
- 1:18–1:37 (everyday problems): And people used AI to solve small problems in everyday life. Not big projects — just real things that get stuck in daily life.
- 1:37–1:56 (evening): The rain kept falling, and nobody wanted to leave.
- 1:56–2:00 (close): In the end, everyone stood together for a big group photo. Here, there is always someone to catch your question. These 2 minutes were picked by Inkling 2 Small from a full day of footage.

## 12. The loop — and what your picks become

This is not a one-off cut. It is a reusable loop: video in,
transcript + keyframes, you pick the moments, ffmpeg cuts them, recap
out. The same loop runs on any video after this one — vlogs included.
The only thing that changes per video is the priority list in §4 and
the music in §7; the loop itself stays the same.

Your picks are saved as training examples (each pick: transcript
excerpt + keyframes + your start/end + title + reason) and used to
LoRA fine-tune Inkling 2 Small, so the cutter learns this taste and
gets better every time it runs. Pick as if you are teaching: prefer
clean, complete moments with a clear start and end and a reason that
would still make sense to someone cutting a different video. Mark
anything uncertain instead of guessing — a wrong pick taught as
correct is worse than no pick at all.

Input: the full transcript with timestamps, plus keyframes from the
video (one frame per second, dense — 1 fps is the workable density;
30 fps would be ~33,000 frames and is not usable).

## 13. Shot taxonomy — label every frame into one of 7 types

When you look at a frame, say which type it is. This is how the cut
gets its coverage (wide → tight) instead of a pile of mid-shots.

1. Storefront ultra-wide — the whole storefront + the whole poster/banner, from outside.
2. Secondary wide — one screen or one poster filling the frame.
3. Interior ultra-wide — the whole room in one frame (presenter + screen + audience).
4. Interior person close-up — one person, close.
5. Interior audience close-up — the audience, close (faces, reactions).
6. Hands/items close-up — hands, a cup, a laptop, a detail.
7. Timelapse — a sped-up passage of time.

If a frame is motion-blurred or unreadable, say "uncertain" instead of
guessing. If a category has no frames in this footage (e.g. no
timelapse), say so — do not invent one.

## 14. Transitions and story — how the shots connect

The recap is a vlog, not a montage of hard cuts. Order the picks so
the day reads front to back, and connect them like this:

- Open on a wide (type 1 or 3) so the viewer knows where they are,
  then go tighter. After a run of close-ups, re-establish with a wide
  so the viewer does not get lost.
- Hide cuts inside motion: cut while someone is turning, gesturing, or
  standing up (match on action), not while they are standing still.
- Bridge with sound: let the next shot's audio start a beat before the
  picture changes (J-cut), or let the current shot's audio run over
  the next picture (L-cut). Do not leave dead silence at a cut.
- Cut to the listener, not the speaker: when someone says something
  funny or surprising, the next shot is the room laughing, not the
  speaker's face.
- Save the tightest close-up for the emotional peak (the room laughing
  together). Do not spend it early.
- Cut on music phrase boundaries (see §7: every 19.4 s for "Timing").
  A cut that lands mid-phrase feels like an interruption; a cut on the
  boundary feels like a breath.
- Photos are 1–2 second flashbacks at the end of a time node, never
  long holds. A photo that sits for seconds drags the pace.

The story arc: the day opens (wide, morning light, people arriving) →
talks and demos unfold, each with a room response → the warmest moment
(the room laughing together) is the peak → a closing wide + photo
flashbacks. The viewer should feel the room getting warmer as the day
goes on.
